# Exercise-name glossary: European Portuguese (`pt`) and Swedish (`sv`)

The terms `catalogue/i18n/pt.json` and `catalogue/i18n/sv.json` use for exercise names, so the
same English word reads the same way across all 5,632 names. Names are stored lower-case like
the English ones (the app capitalises them). Keep `v. 2`, numbers, degrees and proper names
(Arnold, Zercher, Pallof, Jefferson, Zottman, Scott, Smith, Copenhagen, sanskrit pose names...)
as they are.

The rule of thumb: write what someone says on the gym floor in Lisbon or Gothenburg, not a
word-for-word copy of the English. Word order follows the language: the movement first, then
the variant, then the equipment.

## Portuguese (Portugal)

European Portuguese, never Brazilian: `de pé` (not "em pé"), `pega` (not "pegada"), `gémeos`
(not "panturrilha"), `anca` (not "quadril"), `peso morto` (not "levantamento terra"), `curl`
(not "rosca"), `elevações` (not "barra fixa"), `aberturas` (not "crucifixo"), `abdómen`,
`chão`, `ioga`, `ginásio`.

## Swedish

Swedish gyms mix Swedish and English. Use the Swedish word where lifters do (`bänkpress`,
`knäböj`, `marklyft`, `utfallssteg`, `armhävningar`, `rodd`, `latsdrag`, `vadpress`, `höftlyft`)
and keep the loans they actually use (`chins`, `pull-ups`, `dips`, `face pulls`, `flyes`,
`curl`, `crunches`, `situps`, `kettlebell swing`, `good mornings`, `burpees`, `shrugs`,
`jumping jacks`).
Swedish writes compounds as one word: `hantelpress`, `kabelrodd`, `bicepscurl`.

# The research behind the Coach

A change the Coach suggests can carry a tag, and the tag links to its section on this page.

This page is the same list the app uses. It is copied from `api/coach/core/evidence.js`, so what you read here is what the Coach is allowed to cite. It is built on the coAIch evidence review, which first collected and checked these studies.

## Grades

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| SR/MA | Systematic review or meta-analysis. Many studies pooled. |
| Review | A narrative review or overview. Studies summarised, not pooled. |
| RCT | Randomised controlled trial. One study, people split at random. |
| OBS | Observational. Watched what people did, no random split. |
| P | Position stand or expert consensus. |
| our rule | A number no study backs. It is a coaching choice, and we say so. |

Studies are mostly in trained or young adults, and averages hide big differences between people. Treat these as a sensible default, not a law.

## volume

What the Coach does with it: more weekly sets help, with diminishing returns.

| Source | Grade | Finding | Link |
|---|---|---|---|
| Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger 2017, *J Sports Sci* 35(11):1073-1082 (online 2016) | SR/MA | More weekly sets gave more growth; even under 5 sets a week still grew. | [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27433992/) |
| Currier et al. 2026, ACSM Position Stand, *Med Sci Sports Exerc* 58(4):851-872 | SR/MA | Size is helped by 10 or more weekly sets; strength by heavier loads and 2 or more sessions a week. | [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/) |
| Pelland et al. 2025, *Sports Med* 56(2):481-505 (print 2026) | SR/MA | Volume helps size and strength with diminishing returns; frequency clearly helped strength and barely size. | [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41343037/) |

## frequency

What the Coach does with it: twice a week per muscle helps strength; size depends on weekly sets, not on how they are spread.

| Source | Grade | Finding | Link |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pelland et al. 2025, *Sports Med* 56(2):481-505 (print 2026) | SR/MA | Volume helps size and strength with diminishing returns; frequency clearly helped strength and barely size. | [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41343037/) |
| Currier et al. 2026, ACSM Position Stand, *Med Sci Sports Exerc* 58(4):851-872 | SR/MA | Size is helped by 10 or more weekly sets; strength by heavier loads and 2 or more sessions a week. | [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/) |
| Schoenfeld, Grgic & Krieger 2019, *J Sports Sci* 37(11):1286-1295 (online 2018) | SR/MA | With equal volume, training frequency does not meaningfully change muscle growth. | [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30558493/) |

## sets-per-exercise

What the Coach does with it: 2 to 4 sets per exercise; more on one exercise adds little.

| Source | Grade | Finding | Link |
|---|---|---|---|

| Cap of 4 sets, 3 for a beginner | our rule | The cap per exercise is our rule; no study sets it. |  |

## effort

What the Coach does with it: big lifts stop 1 to 3 reps short; failure adds fatigue, not growth.

| Source | Grade | Finding | Link |
|---|---|---|---|
| Robinson et al. 2024, *Sports Med* 54(9):2209-2231 | SR/MA | Size gains rise as sets end closer to failure; strength is similar across a wide range of reps in reserve. | [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38970765/) |
| Refalo et al. 2023, *Sports Med* 53(3):649-665 (online 2022) | SR/MA | Going to momentary failure is not better than stopping short for size. | [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36334240/) |
| Refalo et al. 2024, *J Sports Sci* 42(1):85-101 | RCT | In trained lifters, quad growth was the same at failure or 1 to 2 reps short; failure caused more fatigue. | [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38393985/) |

## rep-range

What the Coach does with it: size grows across wide rep ranges; heavy loads for strength.

| Source | Grade | Finding | Link |
|---|---|---|---|
| Schoenfeld, Grgic, Ogborn & Krieger 2017, *J Strength Cond Res* 31(12):3508-3523 | SR/MA | With sets taken to failure, low and high loads give similar growth; high loads give more 1RM. | [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28834797/) |
| Lopez et al. 2021, *Med Sci Sports Exerc* 53(6):1206-1216 | SR/MA | Growth does not depend on load; strength is better with high and moderate loads than with low. | [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33433148/) |

| Source | Grade | Finding | Link |
|---|---|---|---|
